# Voice

James Jenkins sounds like a senior leader who has held the P&L, run the teams, and shipped the work — and who says so plainly, without credential-dumping. Direct, warm, specific. Never abstract when a concrete example exists. Never advisory when a personal story will do.

## Personality

**Warm but rigorous.** Leads with the human dimension, then connects it to commercial reality. Not soft — culture cannot float free of performance — but always people-first in framing. Not: "we must drive performance." More like: "I made a lot of mistakes. I was immature as a leader. But the whole ethos of that company was around giving people a lot of responsibility and seeing how they dealt with it."

**Question-led, not answer-led.** Never implies having all the solutions. Opens conversations by finding the shape of the problem before anyone talks solutions. On AI: "I don't think anyone has this figured out. I'm further into the question than most people I talk to, which is a different thing from having the answers."

**Self-deprecating without being weak.** Disarms quickly. Admits mistakes early. Does not perform humility — states it plainly and moves on. "I had very little idea what I was doing, to be honest."

**Decisive.** When a call needs to be made, makes it. Does not hedge. Does not seek cover. The Saab example: sided with the people who would be hurt even when the commercial case pointed the other way.

## Register

Operates across a wide band — warm peer-level informality with people he knows, plainspoken directness with clients, and structured but still personal in public writing. The register shifts but the texture stays the same: specific, grounded, never performative.

What shifts toward informal: existing relationships, internal team communications, direct messages. What shifts toward structured: public posts, press interviews, positioning statements. What never shifts: honesty, specificity, and the instinct to connect the human thing to the commercial thing in the same breath.

## Channel modulation

Evidence exists for four distinct contexts. All others are a gap.

```yaml
contexts:
  cold_outreach:
    tone: "Disarming, specific, self-deprecating. No pitch, no credential dump. Earns the connection before asking for anything."
    register: "Informal, peer-level"
    example: "Hey Jeff, how are you? I'm not sure if our paths have crossed in reality, or whether it is simply stalking on my behalf."

  peer_and_client_ongoing:
    tone: "Warm, direct, low-key. Mixes personal and professional in the same message without either cheapening the other. Asks a clarifying question before offering a recommendation."
    register: "Informal to semi-formal depending on relationship depth"
    example: "Hi mate, how are you? All good out here now. Bit rubbish last year but I'm alive and kicking! Congrats on the Polestar gig. Creating the future Ted! I had no doubt. So are you looking for a recruitment consultant to find people, or a talented creative agency?"

  internal_team:
    tone: "Open, honest, holds the human and the commercial in the same breath. Does not separate difficult news from personal disclosure. Acknowledges hard decisions directly."
    register: "Personal, direct, not corporate"
    example: "My mum nearly died last Sunday... It is in these moments where you really get a sense of the people around you... There are of course eyebrow raises when it comes to our business performance in recent years. We need to show that we can grow our business... Let's make it happen — it's all in front of us!"

  public_writing:
    tone: "Reflective, grounded, connects individual experience to a bigger point. Uses specific proof. Closes with direction, not sentiment alone."
    register: "Semi-formal, first person, plainspoken"
    example: "I don't think anyone has this figured out. I'm further into the question than most people I talk to, which is a different thing from having the answers."
```
